## The short version

- **No account.** You don't sign up for Calore, and we don't know who you are.
- **Your data stays on your phone:** your diary, recipes, boxes, plans, weight, body measurements, cycle and supplements. We, the people who make Calore, can't see any of it.
- **Food search:** when you search or scan a food online, your phone asks Open Food Facts. It sends only the words or the barcode.
- **Household (optional):** if you create or join a household, the things you share (shopping list, pantry, boxes, recipes, plans, requests) are shared with the other phones in your home over your Wi-Fi, encrypted. Your diary, weight, body and cycle data are never shared.
- **Help research (optional, off by default):** if you switch it on, a weekly summary of the kinds of data *you choose* goes to our research database in the EU. It contains no name, no exact dates and no food names. You can stop at any time and delete your data from the server.
- **We never sell data and never use it for advertising.** The app contains no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

**Android backup:** like many apps, Calore lets Android include its data in your phone's backup and in phone-to-phone transfer. ## 4. Data that leaves your phone

There are only these cases.

### 4.1 Open Food Facts (food search and barcodes)

- **When:** you search for a food online, scan a barcode, or open a product that isn't on your phone yet.
- **What is sent:** the words you typed or the barcode, the language of your phone, the country set in the app (so products sold there come first), and the app's name and version. Nothing about you, your diary or your health.
- **To whom:** Open Food Facts, a non-profit association in France that runs an open food database. Like any website, they see your phone's internet address. See their privacy policy at <https://world.openfoodfacts.org/privacy>.
- **We receive nothing:** your phone talks to Open Food Facts directly.

### 4.2 Household sharing over your home Wi-Fi (only if you use it)

- **When:** you create or join a household (by scanning a QR code), and your phone is on the same Wi-Fi as the household's hub phone.
- **What is shared:** the shopping list, pantry, prep boxes and who owns them, recipes, shops and products, prices, the shared parts of plans, requests ("cook for me", "buy for me", "can I have it?"), an activity feed ("Anna bought eggs"), each member's name and colour, and **only if you allow it**, your meal targets (so someone can cook a portion that fits you).
- **What is never shared:** your diary, weight, body measurements, check-ins, cycle data, supplements and research settings.
- **How:** directly between the phones on your home network, encrypted with a key that only your household's phones have (from the QR code). One phone, the hub, keeps the shared data so the others can sync. **There is no server**, and the data never leaves your home network.
- **Who can see it:** the members of your household.
- **Leaving:** when you leave, the others keep their copies of what was shared before. Delete items you own before leaving if you don't want them to keep them.
- **We receive nothing.**

### 4.3 The research program (only if you agree)

Explained in section 5.

### 4.4 Nothing else

No analytics, no advertising networks, no crash-reporting services, no location tracking. If this ever changes, this policy changes first, and the app will tell you.

## 5. The research program ("Help research")

### 5.1 Why

To understand how people in Czechia eat: for example how much protein and fibre different age groups get, how eating changes during weight loss, how it relates to the menstrual cycle, and how common supplements are. The results are published only as **statistics about groups** (for example "average fibre intake of women aged 25–29"). The data is not used for advertising, is not sold, and is not used to make decisions about any person.

### 5.2 It is your choice

- It is **off** until you switch it on in Me → Help research.
- You must be **18 or older**.
- You choose **each kind of data** separately: *Food and nutrition*, *Body*, *Cycle*, *Supplements*. Cycle data is sent only if you switch on *Cycle* specifically.
- Nothing is chosen for you in advance.
- You can **stop** at any time (section 5.10). Stopping has no effect on how the app works for you.

### 5.3 What we collect

Only weekly **summaries** of the kinds of data you chose. The full list of fields is in [appendix A](#appendix-a-research-report-fields). In short:

| Kind | What is sent |
|---|---|
| Always (when you take part) | A random participant number, the consent version, the kinds of data you chose, your age band (for example 25–29), sex, diet type, goal type, activity level, and, if you choose, your region (kraj) |
| Food and nutrition | Per week: number of logged days, average calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, fibre and sugar per day, meals per day, your calorie target, which food groups your calories came from (in %), how much came from prep boxes, how closely you followed your plan, the number of boxes thrown away |
| Body | Your BMI band, the change of your weight trend per week (rounded to 0.1 kg), your body-fat band if measured |
| Cycle | For each finished cycle: its length, the period length, the type of contraception (none, hormonal, copper IUD), how many days had each symptom in each phase, average calories per phase. **No dates.** |
| Supplements | Which kinds you take (for example vitamin D) and how regularly (in %) |

**We never collect:** your name, email or phone number; exact dates or times (only the week number); the names of foods, recipes or products; barcodes; notes or any text you wrote; photos; your location (only the region, if you choose); device or advertising identifiers; data about other people in your household or people you cook for; prices or money.

### 5.4 How it works

- When you agree, the app creates a **random participant number** and a **secret key** on your phone. The server stores the number and only a scrambled (hashed) version of the key.
- Every Monday the app builds the summary of the previous week, stores it on your phone, and sends it over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).
- **See what we send** (in the app) shows every report exactly as it was or will be sent.
- The server stores the week number of each report, not the time it arrived, and **does not store your internet address**.

### 5.5 Legal basis

- Your **consent** (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)), and for health data, including cycle data, your **explicit consent** (Article 9(2)(a)).
- Because this is scientific research, we apply the safeguards of Article 89(1): a random participant number instead of your identity (pseudonymisation), collecting only summaries (data minimisation), and publishing only group statistics.

### 5.6 Who can access it

- [Name], the controller, and [no one else at the moment / named research collaborators, if any].
- Our hosting provider, [provider name], which stores the data on our behalf under a data processing agreement (Article 28) and may not use it for anything else.
- Nobody else. Published results only ever describe **groups of at least 10 people**.

### 5.7 Where it is stored

On servers in the European Union: [provider, country]. The data is not transferred outside the EU or the European Economic Area.

### 5.8 How long

Until you delete it (section 5.10), or **5 years after your last report**, whichever comes first. Then it is deleted. Published group statistics may be kept, because they contain nothing about any single person.

### 5.9 Pseudonymous, not anonymous

We don't know who you are. But a series of weekly health summaries about one person still counts as personal data under the GDPR, so we protect it as such (section 9).

### 5.10 Stopping and deleting

- **Stop sharing** (Me → Help research): no more reports are sent, and reports waiting on your phone are deleted.
- **Delete my data from the server:** the app asks the server to delete your participant record and all your reports. This happens immediately when you are online; if you are offline, the app keeps trying until it works.
- **Before you uninstall the app, delete your data from the server first**, if you want it deleted. Without the app we can't know which data is yours, because we don't know who you are. If you've lost your phone, email us your participant number (shown in the app; you can write it down) and we will delete the data.
- Statistics that were already published can't be changed, but they can't be traced back to you.

### 5.11 Changes

If we want to collect anything new or use the data for a new purpose, the app asks you again. Nothing new is collected until you agree.

## 8. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

- **access** your data (Article 15),
- have it **corrected** (Article 16),
- have it **deleted** (Article 17),
- **restrict** its use (Article 18),
- **receive it** in a portable format (Article 20),
- **object** (Article 21),
- **withdraw your consent** at any time, without affecting what was done before (Article 7(3)),
- **complain** to the data protection authority (Article 77).

**How to use them:**
- **Data on your phone:** use the app itself. You can see, change, export and delete everything. We don't have this data, so we can't do it for you.
- **Research data:** use Me → Help research (see what is sent, stop, delete), or email us at [email address] with your participant number. We answer within one month.

**The Czech data protection authority:**
Úřad pro ochranu osobních údajů (ÚOOÚ), Pplk. Sochora 27, 170 00 Praha 7, <https://uoou.gov.cz/>

## 9. Security

**On your phone:** Android keeps each app's data separate from other apps, and encrypts it while the phone is locked. You can lock the cycle section or the whole app with your fingerprint, face or PIN.

**Household sharing:** every message between household phones is encrypted (AES-256-GCM) with a key that only your household's phones have. The key is protected by the Android Keystore. A phone that is removed from the household gets no new data, because the key is changed.

**Research server:**
- encrypted connections only (HTTPS);
- each participant has a secret key, and the server stores only its hash;
- only summaries are stored, and no internet addresses;
- access is limited to the controller;
- encrypted backups;
- security updates are applied.

**If something goes wrong:** if a breach of the research database is likely to put people's rights at risk, we report it to ÚOOÚ within 72 hours (Article 33). If the risk to participants is high, we inform them (Article 34). Because we don't know who participants are, we do this through a message in the app the next time it contacts the server, and on the project page [project page URL].

## 10. Automated suggestions

Targets, meal plans, portions, freshness warnings and cycle tips are **suggestions** calculated on your phone. They are not decisions with legal or similar effects about you (Article 22 does not apply), and you can always change them.

## 11. Not medical advice

Calore is not a medical device. Calorie targets, body-fat estimates, food-safety dates and cycle tips are general estimates, not medical advice. Cycle predictions must not be used for birth control or for planning a pregnancy. If you have a medical condition, an eating disorder, or are pregnant or breastfeeding, talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian.

## Appendix C: Checklist for the owner before research goes live

- [ ] Fill in the controller's name, address and email, and the hosting provider and country.
- [ ] Choose hosting **in the EU** and sign a data processing agreement with the provider (GDPR Article 28).
- [ ] Do a **data protection impact assessment** (Article 35). Large-scale processing of health data normally requires one; check ÚOOÚ's list of processing that requires an assessment.
- [ ] Keep **records of processing activities** (Article 30).
- [ ] Decide whether a **data protection officer** is needed (Article 37). This becomes likely if the research grows to many participants.
- [ ] Translate this policy and the consent texts into Czech, in plain language, and have them **legally reviewed**.
- [ ] If the research is done with a university, ask its **ethics committee**.
- [ ] Set up security: HTTPS, encrypted backups, limited access, updates, and a written plan for breaches.
- [ ] Test **deletion** end to end before launch.
- [ ] Publish the final policy at a stable address and link it in the app.
